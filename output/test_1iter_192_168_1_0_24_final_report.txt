## Remediation and Recommendations

1. **Perform a Full Service Discovery Scan**  
   *Command:* `nmap -sV -sC -O -p- 192.168.1.0/24`  
   *Goal:* Identify open ports, service versions, and OS fingerprints.

2. **Target Critical Infrastructure First**  
   *Router (192.168.1.1):* Scan ports 80, 443, 22, 23, 53, 8080.  
   *Pi‑hole (192.168.1.152):* Scan ports 80, 443, 53, 22.

3. **Validate Firmware Versions**  
   - Check router and Pi‑hole for the latest firmware.  
   - Verify Blink device and other IoT devices against vendor advisories.

4. **Implement Network Segmentation**  
   - Place IoT devices on a separate VLAN or subnet to limit lateral movement.

5. **Enable Logging and Monitoring**  
   - Ensure all devices send logs to a central SIEM.  
   - Monitor for anomalous traffic patterns.

6. **Regular Vulnerability Scanning**  
   - Schedule quarterly scans of all hosts.  
   - Integrate findings into the incident response plan.

7. **Document Unknown Devices**  
   - Maintain an inventory of all devices, including MAC addresses and physical locations.  
   - Periodically verify that each device is authorized.

## Final Summary

The initial ping‑based reconnaissance of the 192.168.1.0/24 subnet revealed 11 active hosts, including key infrastructure devices such as the router and Pi‑hole. No open ports or services were detected, resulting in a **low overall risk** assessment at this stage. However, the presence of multiple unknown devices and the lack of service data create significant blind spots. To achieve a comprehensive security posture, a full service discovery and vulnerability scan should be conducted immediately, followed by firmware validation, network segmentation, and continuous monitoring. These actions will transform the current low‑risk status into a robust, defensible network environment.
